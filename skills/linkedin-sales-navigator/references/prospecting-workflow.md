# Evidence-based prospect discovery

Use this workflow for requests to find the best prospects, prioritize outreach or spend a limited prospecting budget. Keep it generic: derive the product, market and roles from the current user.

The sequence, comparison method, evidence fields and completion checks below are **skill-authored execution guidance**. They are not official LinkedIn methodology, a built-in score, a guarantee of sales outcomes or proof that all relevant buyers can be found. Do not attribute them to LinkedIn. Do not infer a prospect's pain, budget or interest from documentation about the tool.

## Define the search brief

1. Extract the offering and its supported capabilities from user-provided material or authoritative product sources. Separate established capability from sales claims and proposed use cases.
2. Define what observable company behavior would make the offering relevant. Write a disqualifier as well: the company sells a service rather than the relevant product, the deployment is incompatible, or the stated requirement is already satisfied.
3. Record must-haves separately from exploratory assumptions and preferences. Specify whether geography means HQ, the contact's location, the customer market or a combination. Do not silently substitute one for another.
4. Distinguish target count, account count, paid-message ceiling and send authorization. “Use up to 20 credits” is neither “find exactly 20 people” nor permission to send. Reuse existing constraints; ask only for missing information that prevents useful work.
5. Choose task mode: discovery, supplied-target research or explanation. If native access is unavailable, prepare the brief and filters and label any external research provisional. Do not claim to have run a search.

## Discover and compare in Sales Navigator

### Establish capability and provenance

Inspect plan and relevant live controls. Record each useful feature as **used**, **available but not used (reason)**, **unavailable (observed reason)** or **not checked**. A menu item alone establishes neither eligibility for a particular record nor actual use. Do not enable services, modify account-wide settings or purchase an upgrade just to complete research.

Use the workbook to record every substantive search: purpose, native search URL when available, included/excluded values, keyword string, selected persona, observed result count, any query adjustment, inspection scope and retained/rejected candidate IDs. Record “count not shown” when appropriate; do not estimate inspected candidates from the total result count.

### Run discovery rather than reverse lookup

Choose the starting point from the business question:

- **Account-first:** discover organizations meeting company-level criteria, verify fit, then find people at those organizations.
- **Lead-first:** discover relevant roles or network paths, then verify their current employers against company criteria.

Both routes require company and person qualification. Use known companies as seeds only when useful; mark their origin. A series of exact-name searches on a manually assembled company list is target research, not open discovery.

Start with a small set of relevant filters, inspect results and refine. Use supported Boolean fields only; retrieve the [Boolean note](articles-prospecting.md#a168061) for syntax. Check inclusions, exclusions, persona changes, spelling corrections and broadened matches in the actual results. Never relax a user must-have just to fill a list.

### Compare an alternative search before declaring best matches

Run a baseline for fit and at least one meaningful alternative that could change selection: another justified segment, a relevant stakeholder role, an available relationship criterion, or a relevant recent-update criterion. If the eligible universe is exhausted or a feature is blocked, record that evidence instead of inventing a second pass. This comparison requirement is this skill's quality control, not a LinkedIn rule.

Keep optional signals separate from hard eligibility. Do not require every candidate to have growth, a recent job change, a post and a mutual connection simultaneously. Compare the union of useful passes and deduplicate by account/profile identity. Keep strong-fit candidates with no visible activity eligible; unknown timing is not failed fit.

Inspect enough distinct results to support the requested selection and explain the inspected scope. Do not fill an arbitrary quota with weak candidates. If the interface limits results, partition relevant searches and record coverage; consult the current search-limit note rather than assuming all matches were reviewed. Stop refining when the requested result is adequately supported, not when every product feature has been opened.

## Verify business fit and choose contacts

For each candidate account, record:

- **Observed fact:** company identity, relevant product/business evidence, source URL and observation date.
- **Relevance hypothesis:** how the user's actual offering could help; state the inference explicitly.
- **Disqualifier or open question:** what could make the account unsuitable, including an existing solution or incompatible requirement.
- **Discovery origin:** search/pass ID, supplied target or external seed.

Use Account IQ when available and relevant, with source checks. Official company product, pricing or technical pages can verify details absent from Sales Navigator. External research supplements native discovery; it must not silently replace it. Do not invent technology stacks, willingness to switch, customer relationships or pain from packaging alone.

For shortlisted accounts, inspect relevant lead search results and available Relationship Explorer recommendations. Use a task-appropriate persona; default seniority is not user intent. Include existing saved leads when comparing stakeholders, since Explorer surfaces unsaved leads.

Compare the plausible problem owner, technical evaluator and economic buyer where those roles exist. Do not invent three people to fill a diagram. Record the selected contact's current employer, title, location and evidence of relevant responsibility. Compare a credible alternative when available and explain why the chosen contact is the better entry point. If role authority is inferred, label it.

Open relationship and activity details for proposed first contacts. Record a visible mutual connection or shared experience and its source without assuming the intermediary will help. If none appears, report “none visible,” not “no relationship exists.” Review the actual content and date before using a post or job move as a hook; unrelated activity is not buying intent. Respect privacy-limited visibility.

Use a Relationship Map when the user needs ongoing account planning or the buying group is complex. Do not create a map merely to claim feature usage. Mark manually assigned stakeholder roles as hypotheses until supported.

## Rank and prepare outreach

Use a transparent comparison, not an invented “Sales Navigator fit score”:

1. Remove verified hard-constraint failures. Put unresolved must-haves in **Needs verification**, outside the qualified shortlist.
2. Compare business fit and relevant responsibility first.
3. Use verified timing and feasible access paths to distinguish similarly suitable prospects. Do not let visibility or a warm path rescue poor fit.
4. Assign **Prioritize**, **Research further**, **Monitor** or **Exclude**, with a reason and uncertainty. These are analyst labels, not LinkedIn statuses. Do not turn them into numerical purchase probabilities.
5. Explain why the leading choices beat credible alternatives within the inspected set. If no timing signal exists, say so; do not manufacture urgency.

Before preparing a paid outreach batch, inspect recipient/message eligibility and current credit balance through supported controls without sending. Distinguish a free route, a paid route and an unverified route. Never equate a Message button with free eligibility. Read the messaging references for current rules.

Draft only from verified business context plus a clearly framed question or hypothesis. Avoid fabricated familiarity and unsubstantiated claims about the user's product. Keep approval state explicit. Follow the SKILL.md requirement for approval of each batch's recipients and text; research, shortlist completion or a credit ceiling never grants send permission.

## Complete and preserve the work

Before claiming completed best-match discovery, verify all of the following:

- Native discovery was executed, with exact criteria and actual inspection scope recorded.
- A meaningful comparison was executed, or a supported reason explains why it could not be.
- Selected accounts satisfy confirmed must-haves; facts and product-fit hypotheses are separated.
- Entry contacts have current role checks and a reason for selection over available alternatives.
- Relevant relationship/timing checks are recorded as observed, none visible, unavailable or unchecked. Unchecked material evidence keeps the affected result provisional.
- The ranking, exclusions and remaining unknowns are inspectable. Do not claim “best in the market”; describe the searched universe.
- Any claimed saved list, search or map is verified in the product. Any outreach is separately authorized and its actual state reported.

Preserve criteria and results in the workbook or an equivalent artifact so another session can resume without repeating research. If saving entities/lists is authorized, use a dedicated working set without overwriting unrelated lists. Saved-search notification settings and sharing have separate consequences; follow current user and tool permissions. If they cannot be changed, retain exact criteria locally and continue qualification. Do not block research on optional setup.

Finish with a concise explanation of what Sales Navigator actually contributed, the strongest supported matches, why others were excluded, and what remains uncertain. Do not return only a plan, a feature inventory or apologies when execution is authorized. If blocked, state the precise blocker and completed independent work without calling the campaign finished.
